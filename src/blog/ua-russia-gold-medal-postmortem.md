# {{ title }}

<p>So, <a href="/blog/ua-how-many-gold-medals-will-russia-win-in-the-2014-winter-olympics/" title="">we nailed our gold medal prediction for Russia</a> in the 2014 Sochi Winter Olympics (exactly 13 gold medals).  That's pretty cool, right?</p>
<p>We won't spend too much time bragging.  Rather, we'll just update the graphs we used last time to close the loop.</p>
<p>Russia's topline medal performance:</p>
<figure><img alt="" loading="lazy" src="/blog/img/ua/russia-gold-medal-postmortem/3737089_orig.png"/></figure>
<p>What a turnaround!  This was their most medals -- gold or otherwise -- since Russia started competing as Russia.  And even though there were more events this time around, they captured a higher share of gold medals than any Olympics in sixteen years:</p>
<figure><img alt="" loading="lazy" src="/blog/img/ua/russia-gold-medal-postmortem/2679323_orig.png"/></figure>
<p>Finally, we'll update our "host country lift chart".  Russia had the strongest lift in performance vs. previous Olympics in a generation of Olympic games:</p>
<figure><img alt="" loading="lazy" src="/blog/img/ua/russia-gold-medal-postmortem/8977607_orig.png"/></figure>
<p><a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Winter_Olympics" rel="noopener" target="_blank">South Korea is the host in 2018</a> (Pyeongchang).  If this site still exists in four years, maybe we'll do a forecast for them!  They won 3% of the medals this time around (<a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Winter_Olympics_medal_table" rel="noopener" target="_blank">3 out of 99 events</a>), so if you had to ask me now, I'd expect a lift similar to what Japan saw in 1998, putting them in the 6% - 8% of gold medals range.</p>
