# {{ title }}

<p>The Madden video game cover is always a hot topic.  It's the most prestigious sports game cover, at least in this country, and <a href="http://espn.go.com/sportsnation/feature/madden25cover" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">ESPN has been running a fan vote</a> (March Madness-style) for the past three years in order to determine who wins the honor.  We even had an office pool, just for fun, and Chris and I both recovered from rough starts to take the top two spots, having both predicted Barry Sanders to beat Adrian Peterson in the final:</p>
<figure><img alt="" loading="lazy" src="/blog/img/ua/the-madden-curse-from-a-different-angle/1913933-474.png"/></figure>
<p>That's all fun and games, but the biggest Madden cover topic is definitely the <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madden_NFL#Madden_Curse" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">Madden Curse</a>.  Surely you are familiar with it at this point, but the basic idea is that the player who is chosen for the Madden cover each year is "cursed" to have a sub-par season, get injured, or both.</p>
<p>There have been countless articles detailing whether this is a "real" impact or just simple regression to the mean, since the cover athlete is usually a player who just had a great season.  So I don't have a ton to add to this ... at least not right now.  (Hey, we've got to save some good stuff for next year, right?)</p>
<p>I just thought it might be worthwhile to explore a slightly different angle.  All of the Madden Curse analysis is on the player's statistical contributions and his health, but I thought it might be equally interesting to see what happens to his respective team's performance in the year he's on the cover.  Does this "curse" impact the whole team?</p>
<p>In a word, yes.</p>
<figure><img alt="" loading="lazy" src="/blog/img/ua/the-madden-curse-from-a-different-angle/8822717_orig.png"/></figure>
<p>All but one team failed to advance further in the cover year as they did in the previous year, and <em>none </em>of these teams <em>won</em> more playoff games in the cover year as in the previous year!  (Only the 2006 Seahawks and 2009 Cardinals even won a single playoff game in the cover year.)</p>
<p>Now, of course, there's still a "regression to the mean" factor at play here, one probably one worth exploring further down the line -- either for this or for a broader question.</p>
<p>But the bottom line here is clear.  It's not just the player on the cover who is cursed -- it's all of his teammates too.</p>
<p>So if you're on Barry Sanders's flag football team this year ... get ready to lose.</p>
