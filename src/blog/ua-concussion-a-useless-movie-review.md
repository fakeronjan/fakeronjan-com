# {{ title }}

<p><em>Concussion</em> isn't a movie about concussions.</p>
<p>Medically, it's about how <em>sub-concussive</em> hits, the everyday plays in football, cause brain damage in the form of <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_traumatic_encephalopathy" rel="noopener" target="_blank">CTE</a>. Structurally, it's about the life of <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennet_Omalu" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Dr. Bennet Omalu</a>, who discovered this link. And societally, it's about America's collective apathy towards this, despite the evidence.</p>
<p>Given all this, perhaps the movie really is about a concussion: the concussion-like daze that our culture is in, unsure of how to react to the revelations that <a href="http://www.profootballhof.com/news/howie-long-s-enshrinement-speech/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">America's national passion</a> is killing its heroes.</p>

<p>My earliest memories of football come from January of 1990, when my then-hometown Colorado Buffaloes made it to the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Orange_Bowl" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Orange Bowl</a> and the Denver Broncos followed that up by making - and losing - <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXIV" rel="noopener" target="_blank">another Super Bowl</a>. I was hooked.</p>
<p>For the next 23 years, my Sundays would be consumed by the NFL, with small breaks for orchestra practice in <a href="http://ccm.uc.edu/prep/music/ensemble/cjs.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">CJS</a> and <a href="http://www.cincinnatisymphony.org/csyo" rel="noopener" target="_blank">CSYO</a>. Even though we had moved to Cincinnati, I continued to root for the Denver Broncos, and this turned out to be a good decision. The Bengals were <a href="http://www.pro-football-reference.com/play-index/tgl_finder.cgi?request=1&amp;match=combined&amp;year_min=1991&amp;year_max=2000&amp;game_type=R&amp;playoff_round=&amp;game_num_min=0&amp;game_num_max=99&amp;week_num_min=0&amp;week_num_max=99&amp;game_day_of_week=&amp;game_time=&amp;time_zone=&amp;game_location=&amp;surface=&amp;roof=&amp;stadium_id=&amp;temperature_gtlt=lt&amp;temperature=&amp;game_result=&amp;overtime=&amp;league_id=&amp;team_id=&amp;team_conf_id=&amp;team_div_id=&amp;opp_id=&amp;opp_conf_id=&amp;opp_div_id=&amp;team_off_scheme=&amp;team_def_align=&amp;opp_off_scheme=&amp;opp_def_align=&amp;conference_game=&amp;division_game=&amp;tm_is_playoff=&amp;opp_is_playoff=&amp;tm_is_winning=&amp;opp_is_winning=&amp;tm_scored_first=&amp;tm_led=&amp;tm_trailed=&amp;tm_won_toss=&amp;c1stat=&amp;c1comp=gt&amp;c1val=&amp;c2stat=&amp;c2comp=gt&amp;c2val=&amp;c3stat=&amp;c3comp=gt&amp;c3val=&amp;c4stat=&amp;c4comp=gt&amp;c4val=&amp;c5comp=&amp;c5gtlt=lt&amp;c6mult=1.0&amp;c6comp=&amp;order_by=points_diff&amp;order_by_asc=Y" rel="noopener" target="_blank">the worst team of the decade</a> that I lived there, while the Broncos exorcised their playoff demons and won two Super Bowls, <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXII" rel="noopener" target="_blank">the first</a> being one of my favorite games I've ever watched in any sport.</p>
<p>Football was the perfect combination of strategy and execution, of violence and beauty, of brawn and brain, and I went in deep. I played hours of <a href="/blog/ua-countdown-rjs-favorite-nfl-video-games/">NFL video games</a>. I <a href="/blog/ua-countdown-rjs-favorite-nfl-films-music/">listened to</a> - and <a href="/blog/ua-from-the-vault-rj-midi-arrangements-of-old-nfl-themes/">remixed</a> - NFL music. I ran and participated in <a href="/blog/ua-post-mortem-to-a-fantasy-football-career/">several fantasy leagues</a>. I created <a href="/blog/ua-nfl-power-rankings-introducing-olandis/">power rankings formulas</a>. I became a <a href="/blog/img/ua/concussion-a-useless-movie-review/3998765-507.jpg">single-share owner</a>. Hell, <a href="/blog/ua-competitive-balance-in-the-nfl/">I started this site</a> because I wanted an outlet for my NFL passion.</p>
<p>And then <a href="/blog/ua-insufferable-proclamation-monday-so-long-nfl/">I quit</a>. I quit watching the NFL, I quit playing fantasy football, and I quit analyzing football statistics, because football kills its players.</p>
<p>Why am I prattling on about myself instead of talking about the movie? I wanted to show that everyone has a breaking point, a threshold beyond which they will tap out. If I can quit the NFL, being part of its target demographic, almost anyone can if pushed to the limit.</p>

<p>So is <em>Concussion</em> the film it needed to be? Kind of.</p>
<p><strong>If we measure this film as a biopic about Dr. Bennet Omalu, then it is a wonderful success</strong>. Will Smith portrays him convincingly across a wide range of scenes - performing autopsies, discovering a groundbreaking link to brain damage, starting a family, and taking on a force larger than himself that desperately wanted to hide the truth. It's a great story, one that I would highly recommend.</p>
<p>That said, <strong>if we measure this film as a story about brain damage in the NFL, then it is a missed opportunity.</strong> <em>Concussion</em> the movie ends just as the the real world story escalated, with the <a href="http://www.nytimes.com/2012/05/03/sports/football/junior-seau-famed-nfl-linebacker-dies-at-43-in-apparent-suicide.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">suicide of Hall of Famer Junior Seau</a>. We now have <a href="http://www.pbs.org/wgbh/frontline/article/new-87-deceased-nfl-players-test-positive-for-brain-disease/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">dozens of cases</a> of confirmed CTE in deceased football players, there is a link to <a href="http://time.com/3342795/nfl-concussions-alzheimers-dementia/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Alzheimer's and dementia</a>, and <a href="http://mmqb.si.com/2015/06/11/nfl-young-players-retirement-problem" rel="noopener" target="_blank">players have begun to retire early</a>, citing concerns about their long-term health.</p>
<p>By focusing the story on Dr. Omalu, <em>Concussion</em> frames the NFL as villains in a short-term cover-up. The truth is that the NFL is a mere mini-boss, and football itself is the big bad. This is why the Frontline special, <em><a href="http://www.pbs.org/wgbh/frontline/film/league-of-denial/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">League of Denial</a></em>, is more impactful than Concussion - it tells the real story more effectively.</p>
<p>This year, nearly a dozen high school football players <a href="http://mmqb.si.com/mmqb/2015/11/24/high-school-football-deaths-2015" rel="noopener" target="_blank">have died</a>. Dr. Omalu <a href="http://www.nytimes.com/2015/12/07/opinion/dont-let-kids-play-football.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">wrote an op-ed</a> asking parents not to let their children play. A key line in <em>Concussion</em> highlights that if 10% of mothers follow Dr. Omalu's advice, then the NFL as we know it is done.</p>
<p>Perhaps this is the power that this film has. Unlike Frontline, <em>Concussion</em> has the ability to reach a mainstream audience. It <a href="http://bleacherreport.com/articles/2599809-concussion-truths-forced-will-smith-to-confront-feelings-for-football-nfl" rel="noopener" target="_blank">forced Will Smith to confront the role football played in his life</a>. Through his fantastic performance, perhaps he will now force millions of football fans to do the same.</p>
<p>After all, everyone has a breaking point.</p>
