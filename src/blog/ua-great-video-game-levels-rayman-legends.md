# {{ title }}

<p>It's time to step up our game. I've been playing video games my entire life, and I want to include more from that world on this site. As such, I'm kicking off a new feature: <strong>Great Video Game Levels</strong>.</p>
<p><em>Super Mario Maker</em> was my <a href="/blog/ua-lets-talk-game-of-the-years/">2015 game of the year</a>, and it kicked my butt in showing just how difficult game design can be. I've created <a href="https://supermariomakerbookmark.nintendo.net/profile/RJSikdar" rel="noopener" target="_blank">a handful of courses</a>, a few of which I think have some interesting ideas in them, but the real degree of difficulty is in the execution.</p>
<p>For example, I built <a href="https://supermariomakerbookmark.nintendo.net/courses/91EA-0000-00AD-0273" rel="noopener" target="_blank">a level based on <em>Aladdin</em>'s Cave of Wonders</a>. The basic beats are there: an adult wearing purple goes inside a lion's head cave opening, a monkey touches the forbidden treasure, and there is an escape out of fiery lava. But it's missing the sense of refinement and polish that the best video game levels have.</p>
<p>That got me thinking about great side-scrolling levels that I've played in the past, and my mind immediately focused on 2013's <em><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayman_Legends" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Rayman Legends</a></em>, a gorgeous platformer that was among my favorites that year. It was a bit of an unfocused package that had an over-reliance on gimmicky touchscreen levels, but it overcame that with some of <strong>the most fun, memorable, and replay-able music-themed levels in history</strong>.</p>
<p><em>Orchestral Chaos</em> is an absolutely gorgeous marriage of visuals and music:</p>

<p>The brilliant hook of these levels lies in the tightness of the sync between the music and the player's actions. It creates the same rush as one might get in <em>Guitar Hero</em>, but with more varied and creative visual stimulation.</p>
<p><span>These six levels</span> vary in difficulty and theme - some are beautiful, some are epic, others are quirky - but they represent some of the most fun experiences I've ever had in a platformer. I've probably played each of them a hundred times to date (that's me in the recordings above), and I'm sure I'll go back to them in the future.</p>
<p>​They're great.</p>
