# {{ title }}

<p>Two years ago <a href="/blog/ua-stupid-stupid-idea-whos-the-best-street-fighter/">we redefined the scale of useless analysis</a> when we watched several hours of the demo mode in<em> Street Fighter II</em> in order to see which character is the best when controlled by the computer. We followed this up with <a href="/blog/ua-stupid-stupid-idea-a-street-fighter-sequel/">a <em>Street Fighter IV</em> version</a> as well.<br/>​<br/>It's time to bring this concept back, this time for <strong><em>Super Smash Bros. for Wii U</em></strong>!</p>
<p><em>Super Smash Bros. for Wii U</em> is crazy. It has 40 fighters included in the game out of the box, you can unlock and purchase more, and the game even includes an 8-player competitive mode which is just ridiculous mayhem. It's the perfect match for some useless analysis!</p>
<p>I decided that I wanted to test <em>two</em> different elements in the game: 1) who are the best fighters when they're controlled by the computer, and 2) how much better is the computer when it's set to level 9 vs. when it's set to lower levels? This second element is easily testable because <em>Smash</em> allows you to set the CPU's skill level between 1 - total newb - and 9 - expert.</p>
<p>So given several dozen fighters and multiple CPU levels to test, here's how I set things up:<ul><li>Set up a series of 8-player CPU battles</li><li>Each battle will be a stock battle with 3 lives: 1st place goes to the last fighter standing, 2nd place goes to the 2nd-to-last fighter standing, and so on</li><li>In each match, there will be one CPU set to level 9, one to level 8, and so on down to level 2 (I decided not to test level 1)</li><li>Fighters and stages will be completely random</li><li>Only the original 40 unlocked fighters are included in the simulations</li><li>I watched <em>90 rounds</em> of these 8-player battles, which took around 10 hours to do across a few weeks (I originally was targeting 200 rounds but decided that would take way too long)</li></ul> All in all, I think this was the most research put into an analysis in the history of this site! (Or perhaps as my wife would characterize it, the most time wasted?)</p>
<p>So what did we find out? Let's start with CPU skill level. Here's a breakdown of the 90 matches into winners and top-3-finishes:</p>
<figure><img alt="" loading="lazy" src="/blog/img/ua/stupid-stupid-idea-a-smash-bros-spectacular/cpu-level-graph_1_orig.jpg"/></figure>
<p>As we'd expect, the higher CPU levels perform better than the lower CPU levels with randomized fighters. Some of our takeaways:<ul><li>72 of the 90 battles (80%) finished with CPU 7, 8, or 9 winning</li><li>There's a big dropoff from CPU level 9 down to 8, and a bigger one from CPU level 7 down to 6</li><li>CPU level 6 is the only one with fewer wins and top 3 finishes than a lower level fighter</li><li>Every CPU level won at least once - although CPU level 2 got its win in round #89 out of 90!</li></ul> ​It's unclear to me how the game processes different levels. Do higher level CPUs try to target their biggest potential threats? That might explain why CPU 6 struggled while CPU 5 slipped under the radar.</p>
<p>It's also possible that CPU 6 simply had some bad luck - either with the random fighters it was assigned or just plain random variation - and extending the simulation would show that CPU 6 is truly better than CPU 5.</p>
<p>From here on out, we'll calculate each CPU's "average finish", which is simply the average of the rank placement across the 90 rounds:</p>
<p><u>Average Finish (lower is better)</u><br/>CPU 9: <strong>3.02</strong><br/>CPU 8: <strong>3.73</strong><br/>CPU 7: <strong>3.67</strong> <em>(better than CPU 8!)</em><br/>CPU 6: <strong>4.58</strong><br/>CPU 5: <strong>4.42</strong><em> (better than CPU 6!)</em><br/>CPU 4: <strong>5.02</strong><br/>CPU 3: <strong>5.58</strong><br/>CPU 2: <strong>5.63</strong></p>
<p>This is ​basically the same story as the wins / top-3 graph, but we need these figures in order to move onto the next phase of the analysis, identifying the best characters!</p>
<p>So we have have a few challenges when evaluating the fighters. Since characters were randomly assigned, not every character was chosen an equal amount of times, and there <em>definitely</em> is not guarantee that every character had the same number of attempts as CPU 9 as they did as CPU 2.</p>
<p>Ultimately, I decided to look at the data several ways:<ol><li>Win %</li><li>Average finish</li><li>A +/- rating that compares to their expected finish based on the CPU levels they were assigned</li></ol> #1 and #2 were easy to compile since they're basically the same as metrics above.</p>
<p>​For #3, I set up a simple weighting function where I took the distribution of CPU levels for each fighter and multiplied it by the average finishes for each of the CPU levels above. So if a fighter was <em>always</em> assigned to CPU 9 we'd expect them to average a finish of 3.02. If that fighter's actual finish was a 2.0, then they finished 1.02 points better than expected and therefore would have a +/- rating of 1.02.</p>
<p>On to the results table!</p>
<figure><img alt="" loading="lazy" src="/blog/img/ua/stupid-stupid-idea-a-smash-bros-spectacular/fighter-rank-graph_2_orig.jpg"/></figure>
<p>Some highlights that I found interesting:<ul><li>I certainly never expected <strong>Kirby</strong> to be #1! And he's way out in front of #2, <strong>Mega Man</strong>!</li><li>I love that <strong>Toon Link</strong> (#3) beat regular <strong>Link</strong> (#5), although he has a much lower win %</li><li>I always made fun of people who chose <strong>Mario</strong> (#4), but it seems like that's actually a pretty justified choice</li><li><strong>Shulk</strong> (#11) ends up below <strong>Palutena</strong> (#10) despite a win % that is substantially higher - this is because Shulk had stronger CPU levels on average than Palutena did (2nd-highest for Shulk, 35th for Palutena)<ul><li>I have never played a game that had either of these characters in it, so <em>Smash</em> is my introduction to them!</li></ul></li><li><strong>Little Mac </strong>(#36) was top 5 based on his win %, but was ultimately very boom-or-bust - much like his trademark Star Punch</li><li>I'm sad that <strong>Zero Suit Samus </strong>finished in last place, but at least normal <strong>Samus</strong> (#24) semi-redeemed the franchise</li></ul>Calling back to the original question, what's a bigger factor - CPU skill or the character selected? Well, the d<span>ifference between Kirby and Zero Suit Samus is (1.45 + 1.09) = 2.54. That's </span><em>nearly</em><span> the difference in average finish between CPU 2 and CPU 9 of (5.63 - 3.02) = 2.61. So it looks like they're basically equal factors: Kirby at CPU level 2 and Zero Suit Samus at CPU level 9 would be expected to be comparable to each other.</span></p>
<p>This exceptionally useless exercise was fun, and it demonstrates to me that watching video games play themselves can continue to be productive. Now, let's close it out on a Kirby dance. Great job little guy!!</p>
