# {{ title }}

<p>There may be snow on the ground in New England right now, but baseball season begins!  And with the start of a new season comes a critical dilemma that I'm wrestling with.</p>
<p><em>Should I raise my son as a Mets fan or a Yankees fan?</em></p>
<p>Baseball is arguably the most magical sport growing up as a kid.  There are games on almost every day, so it's fun to track and talk about.  The stats are a big part of keeping up with the sport, which gets kids engaged with numbers.  And of course, there's nothing like going to the ballpark on a nice summer day and watching your favorite player hit one out of the park.</p>
<p><a href="/blog/ua-box-score-monday-rjs-first-baseball-game/" title="">I grew up a Cincinnati Reds fan</a>, of course, so part of me would love to see Little Man rooting for daddy's favorite team.  But I have to be realistic, too.  He was born in Manhattan and is going to grow up in NYC, and he's going to want to follow a local team.</p>
<p>So, which one should I get him started with?  I've tried breaking this down on several factors:</p>
<p><ul><li>We currently live in the Bronx (going on eight years).  If we stay here, all of my son's friends will be Yankees fans.  <strong>+1 Yankees</strong></li><li>Citi Field is a far better experience than the new Yankee Stadium.  You don't have to be super focused on the baseball when you're at Citi Field.  This is a good thing.  Also, better food.  <strong>+1 Mets</strong></li><li>Yankees fans are reviled nationwide.  However, Mets fans are pitied nationwide.  I feel like the latter is worse.  Site writer Chris is a great example of what Mets fandom can do to you.  <strong>+1 Yankees</strong></li><li>The idea of being a Met(ropolitan) is super appealing, particularly since my son is mixed race.  <strong>+1 Mets</strong></li><li>The Mets are responsible for my worst baseball-related memory, ever.  I attended <a href="http://www.baseball-reference.com/boxes/CIN/CIN199910040.shtml" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">the 1999 play-in game between the Reds and the Mets</a>, and I was sitting in miserably cold weather, in the top row, behind the scoreboard (our writer Mike also attended).  The Mets won, 5-0, leaving <a href="http://www.baseball-reference.com/teams/CIN/1999.shtml" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">the 96-win Reds</a> as one of the best teams ever to not make the playoffs.  <strong>+1 Yankees</strong></li><li>The Yankees have one of the worst contracts in baseball history.  <strong>+1 Mets</strong></li><li>The Mets have one of the worst contracts in baseball history.  <strong>+1 Yankees</strong></li><li>The Yankees are losing icons left and right to retirement.  Mariano's gone, and Jeter's done after this year.  <strong>+1 Mets</strong></li><li>The Mets have no icons to lose.  No, David Wright does not count.  <strong>+1 Yankees</strong></li><li>The Mets have more interesting colors.  I loved orange and blue growing up, as I'm a former Denver Broncos fan.  <strong>+1 Mets</strong></li><li>The Yankees have better uniforms. <strong> +1 Yankees<br/></strong></li></ul></p>

<p><ul><li>My friend Mike (different Mike than the one who writes for us) already bought my son a Mets t-shirt.  <strong>+1 Mets</strong></li></ul></p>

<p><ul><li>I attended the Mets' final loss at Shea Stadium and the Yankees' first victory at the new Yankee Stadium.  Seems like a pretty fair "legacy snapshot" for each franchise.  <strong>+1 Yankees</strong></li></ul></p>
<p><ul><li>The <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Met" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">Mets have a mascot</a> similar to <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Red" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">the Reds' mascot</a>.  Both are hilariously offputting.  <strong>+1 Mets</strong></li><li><a href="/blog/ua-its-been-a-long-time/" title="">The Yankees are just better</a>, and seem likely to remain so.  Do I want to subject my son to a losing legacy?  <strong>+1 Yankees</strong></li><li>Conversely, there's nothing better than rooting for an underdog.  No one roots for the 1% in real life, so why should we do so in sports?  <strong>+1 Mets</strong></li></ul></p>
<p>Well, that settled nothing!  What should I do?</p>
