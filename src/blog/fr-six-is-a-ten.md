# {{ title }}

<p>I saw <a href="https://www.broadway.com/shows/six/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="text-decoration:underline"><em>Six</em></span></a> yesterday, just a few weeks into its opening Broadway run, and it’s the most fun I’ve had in a theater since I saw <em>Hamilton</em> five years ago.</p>
<p>It feels impossible to talk about <em>Six</em> without talking about <em>Hamilton</em>. They’re both global phenomenons that disrupted musical theater via unique historical retellings. They were big enough pop culture sensations to make it onto <em>my</em> radar, an accomplishment given that I’m a casual musical fan who attends roughly one Broadway show each year. And they’re both great, with fantastic cast recordings I will listen to whenever I need a shot of aural energy.</p>
<p>All that said, <em>Six</em> isn’t <em>Hamilton</em>. And that’s okay.</p>
<p><em>Hamilton</em> told the story of America through the primary perspective of one man. <em>Six</em> eschews telling the story of Henry VIII to focus on the six ex-wives we only hear of because of him.</p>
<p><em>Hamilton</em> is sprawling - a two hour deep dive that hits all the key events in Alexander Hamilton’s life. <em>Six </em>is focused, granting us maybe ten minutes with each of its six queens, and short enough to not need an intermission.</p>
<p><em>Hamilton</em> approached diversity on Broadway through deliberately color-blind and color-switch casting. <em>Six</em> approaches diversity by highlighting the wide range of pop music styles, each represented by its own ex-wife and drawing overt inspiration from stars from Beyonce to Avril Lavigne to my favorite Alicia Keys.</p>
<figure><img alt="" loading="lazy" src="/blog/img/fr/six-is-a-ten/img_9214.jpg"/></figure>
<p><em>Hamilton</em> and <em>Six</em> are both incredible, fun, and game-changing musicals, but for completely different reasons.</p>
<p>When I saw <em>Hamilton</em> I had never seen or heard anything like it. It was novel, it was subversive, it brought a new swagger. It was a shock to the system, one that took me weeks to fully absorb and process. Literally - <a href="/blog/fr-hamilton/"><span style="text-decoration:underline">it took me a month to pull together 2,500 words about it</span></a>.</p>
<p>When I saw <em>Six</em>, I felt at home. While I’d never heard any of these songs before … I’d heard them all before. The writers found a way to match each story to a powerful pop music archetype in an instantly recognizable way that I really enjoyed.</p>
<p>Ultimately, the underlying messages of female power and sisterhood threaded throughout in a way we’ve seen many times before. It was an uplifting, raucous 80 minutes that had the crowd amped throughout. It’s amazing, and in the Brexit-and-Trump era, maybe it’s the musical we need.</p>
<p>Go see it!</p>
<p>P.S. - if you’re interested, I’d actually recommend <em>not</em> familiarizing yourself with the songs in advance. Seeing how they match each queen’s look and sound is worth keeping a surprise.</p>
