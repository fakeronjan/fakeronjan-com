# {{ title }}

<p>Christmas has come and gone this year, and we're precariously close to the end of the "holiday music is acceptable right now" season. While we still have the chance, I wanted to finally definitively rank the verses of my favorite holiday song ever, <em>You're A Mean One, Mr. Grinch</em>.</p>
<p>Now, I'm no Scrooge - I love the holiday season and Christmas in particular. This isn't a humbug-style rant against the consumerism that has taken over a season or anything like that. And it's not a fatalistic, lefty "who can be cheery in <em>this</em> version of America" cry for help either.</p>
<p>I just love the song because it's hilarious. It's the best diss track in history. I mean, just listen to this thing!</p>

<p>So with that said, <a href="https://genius.com/13247431" rel="noopener" target="_blank">let's review the insults</a> by verse and then count down to the best of the bunch.</p>
<p><strong>Verse 1:</strong><br/>You're a mean one, Mr. Grinch<br/>You really are a heel<br/>You're as cuddly as a cactus<br/>You're as charming as an eel<br/>Mr. Grinch<br/>You're a bad banana with a greasy black peel</p>
<p><strong>Verse 2:</strong><br/>You're a monster, Mr. Grinch<br/>Your heart's an empty hole<br/>Your brain is full of spiders<br/>You've got garlic in your soul<br/>​Mr. Grinch<br/>I wouldn't touch you with a<br/>Thirty-nine and a half foot pole</p>
<p><strong>Verse 3:</strong><br/>You're a vile one, Mr. Grinch<br/>You have termites in your smile<br/>You have all the tender sweetness of a seasick crocodile<br/>Mr. Grinch<br/>Given the choice between the two of you<br/>I'd take the seasick crocodile</p>
<p><strong>Verse 4</strong><br/>You're a foul one, Mr. Grinch<br/>You're a nasty wasty skunk<br/>Your heart is full of unwashed socks<br/>Your soul is full of gunk<br/>Mr. Grinch<br/>The three best words that best describe you<br/>Are as follows, and I quote<br/>Stink<br/>Stank<br/>Stunk</p>
<p><strong>Verse 5</strong><br/>You're a rotter Mr. Grinch<br/>You're the king of sinful sots<br/>Your heart's a dead tomato splotched with moldy purple spots<br/>Mr. Grinch<br/>Your soul is an appalling dump heap<br/>Overflowing with the most disgraceful assortment of deplorable rubbish imaginable<br/>Mangled up in tangled up knots</p>
<p><strong>Verse 6</strong><br/>You nauseate me, Mr. Grinch<br/>With a nauseous super "naus"<br/>You're a crooked jerky jockey and you drive a crooked horse<br/>Mr. Grinch<br/>You're a three-decker sauerkraut and toadstool sandwich<br/>With arsenic sauce</p>
<p>And now the countdown!</p>
<p><strong>#6: Verse 1 ("You're a mean one")</strong><br/>This is a strong opening statement, but you know the song is holding back the good stuff.</p>
<p><strong>#5: Verse 4 ("You're a foul one")</strong><br/>This is definitely the most smell-dominant of the verses and the skunk-gunk-stunk rhyme is probably the best in the whole song.</p>
<p><strong>#4: Verse 6 ("You nauseate me")<br/></strong>Now we're getting vicious. Going after the character of the Grinch's horse?! Savage! And the "with arsenic sauce" closer is brutal. Not gonna lie, though, a sauerkraut and toadstool sandwich <em>without</em> the arsenic could be good, right? RIGHT?</p>
<p><strong>#3: Verse 3 ("You're a vile one")</strong><br/>I love the "termites in your smile" line so much. For some bizarre reason, I always expect the termites to be in his heart or soul or mind based on the other lines. And yes, it's a bit of a cheat to rhyme "crocodile" with itself, but I love both the idea of a seasick crocodile and the notion that someone would prefer one to anything else.</p>
<p><strong>#2: Verse 2 ("You're a monster")<br/></strong>Just gold end-to-end. "Heart's an empty hole", "brain is full of spiders", and "garlic in your soul" are so wonderfully descriptive. I do wonder if a 40-foot-pole is long enough to touch the Grinch. He's leaving the door open on it!<strong></strong></p>
<p>#1: Verse 5 ("You're a rotter")<br/>"Your soul is an appalling dump heap overflowing with the most disgraceful assortment of deplorable rubbish imaginable" is already a Hall of Fame quality burn. Adding in "mangled up in tangled up knots" makes it transcendent. This is the best insult of all time and the best verse in the best holiday song ever.</p>
