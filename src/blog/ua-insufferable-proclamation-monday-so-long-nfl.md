# {{ title }}

<p>I had originally planned two posts for today:</p>
<p><ol><li>A Box Score Monday telling the story of <a href="http://www.pro-football-reference.com/boxscores/200010220cin.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">the Broncos-Bengals game that I went to back in 2000</a> when Corey Dillon broke the single-game rushing yardage record (22 carries, 278 yards) and I sat him on my bench in fantasy football for <a href="http://www.pro-football-reference.com/players/A/AutrDa00.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">Darnell Autry</a>.</li><li>An Eli Manning "tribute" post where I asked, quite literally, "are we sure that Eli Manning is actually good?"  I compared him to the ten other quarterbacks with multiple Super Bowl wins using stats from game logs, finding that he's a solid QB with a weaker "A game" than his counterparts.</li></ol></p>
<p>So, what happened?  Well ... I'm going noble.  I'm done with the NFL.  For good.  Starting tomorrow.</p>
<p>These are the types of posts that I suspect are much more fun to write than they are to read, and for that I apologize in advance.  And I'm fully aware that my opinions are no more valid than anyone else's and no one was asking for a preachy, sanctimonious rant.  But too bad, I'm doing it anyway!</p>
<p>So, seriously -- why am I quitting this game I've loved for so long?  There are lots of reasons, I suppose.</p>
<p><ul><li>The game is too violent, leading to too many serious injuries.  <a href="http://www.washingtonpost.com/blogs/early-lead/wp/2013/10/21/nfl-injuries-on-a-brutal-sunday-brian-cushing-sam-bradford-jay-cutler-lead-list/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">Just yesterday</a>, we had major injuries to Brian Cushing, Jay Cutler, Sam Bradford, and Reggie Wayne.  This seriously impacts the quality of the games we see and the context for the season.</li><li>It's sadly becoming clear that even beyond these injuries, <a href="http://espn.go.com/espn/otl/story/_/id/9745797/new-book-league-denial-says-nfl-used-resources-power-two-decades-deny-football-link-brain-damage" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">players are basically causing themselves brain damage just by playing the sport</a>.  (Watch the <a href="http://www.pbs.org/wgbh/pages/frontline/league-of-denial/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">full Frontline episode of League of Denial</a> if possible.)  I don't feel comfortable supporting that.</li><li>The NFL knows this, but didn't care until there were legal and financial implications.  Even now, they speak of <a href="http://profootballtalk.nbcsports.com/2013/09/23/nfl-resumes-quiet-push-for-18-game-schedule/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">lengthening the season to 18 games</a>.</li><li>NFL ownership teams insist on <a href="http://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2013/10/how-the-nfl-fleeces-taxpayers/309448/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">socializing the costs of running teams while privatizing the profits</a>.  In a product like this where the labor <em>is the product</em>, I feel that the ownership model does little but extract money from a community.  Just look at the Green Bay Packers as an example of how sports teams can better operate -- and one that, sadly, <a href="http://www.newyorker.com/online/blogs/sportingscene/2011/01/those-non-profit-packers.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">the NFL will not allow in other cities</a>.</li><li>The <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NFL_lockout" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">2011 NFL lockout</a> was one of the greediest money grabs I have ever seen.</li><li><a href="http://www.businessinsider.com/small-amount-of-money-from-pink-nfl-merchandise-goes-to-breast-cancer-research-2013-10" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">The league profits off of Breast Cancer Research merchandise</a>.  This feels wrong to me, even if it's a small share of dollars.  (I run a scholarship fund myself, and I would never take profits off of our merchandise.)</li><li>Many football fans are drunk, fat assholes. :)</li></ul></p>
<p>You may not agree with some or perhaps any of these reasons, and that's completely fair.  Again, this is a personal choice.</p>
<p>How serious am I about this?  Well, I divorced the Denver Broncos when they fired Mike Shanahan, Jay Cutler, and Brandon Marshall after the 2008 season.  I haven't gone back to them.</p>
<p>It's going to be harder to quit the whole league, after 25 years of being a superfan.  But I think I can do it.  Here's what this means, both for me and this site:</p>
<p><ul><li>No more covering the NFL, personally, for the site.  All of our other writers can cover the NFL as much as they want, though.</li><li>We are moving Box Score Monday from a weekly feature to a "whenever we feel like it" feature, since most of the box scores I had lined up were football games.  Chris's ADAMLE NCAA Football power rankings will move to Mondays.</li><li>The OLANDIS rankings will continue, but I will not run them or post the articles anymore.</li><li>The hardest part will be quitting fantasy football, but <a href="http://www.slate.com/articles/sports/sports_nut/2013/10/fantasy_football_how_the_game_within_a_game_keeps_fans_rabidly_uncritically.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="">that's arguably the most important step</a>.  I'm in a personal high 5 leagues this year, and I'll play in zero next year.</li><li>I will continue to be a Green Bay Packers owner.  Community ownership is still important!</li><li>I'll need to find a replacement summer-fall sport when the NBA isn't in-season.  Maybe soccer!</li></ul></p>
<p>So that's it.  Now, time to get ready to go to the Monday Night Football game tonight between the 0-6 Giants and the 1-4 Vikings!  (That's why I don't officially quit until tomorrow. :D)</p>
<p>-rj</p>
